iPad practice app · personal project

Hear every stroke. See which ones were late.

DrumCoach listens to a practice pad through the iPad mic, or to an e-kit over MIDI, while you play along to a click. Each note turns green when it's on time, amber when it's early or late, and red when it's missed. I built it for myself while working through Stick Control toward Rockschool Grade 8.

A practice session

From “what do I practise today” to a number you can track

  1. Today picks the work

    A short list for the day, sized to the time you have: Stick Control style stickings, rudiments, a level test. Choose pad, kit or both, and kit-only exercises turn into their hand part on the pad.

  2. A spoken count-in

    “One, two, three, four” on the click, then the notation starts moving. The playhead runs through the bar, R/L sits under each note, and the two-lane strip shows right hand on top, left below.

  3. Every note gets a colour

    The app matches each stroke to the nearest note and shows how many milliseconds off it was. Amber marks sit before the note when you rushed and after it when you dragged.

  4. A score and a diagnosis

    You get a percentage for the run, plus where it went wrong: which hand, which part of “1 e & a”, and whether you drift. A constant offset usually means latency to calibrate; an offset that grows through the run means rushing or dragging.

  5. Kept forever

    Every run is saved from the first session on, with no time limit, so “last time: 50 BPM, 79%” and the long-term charts are always there.

Input

It listens to what you actually play

Practice pad · mic

Eight taps to set it up

The setup measures the room's noise floor, your softest stroke and the brightness of a stick hit, so talking and fridge hum don't count as notes. Latency is stored per output device, and the app warns you about Bluetooth headphones, which add too much delay.

E-kit · MIDI

USB or Bluetooth MIDI

Plug in a USB-MIDI cable or pair a Bluetooth MIDI adapter from Settings. Roland note numbers work out of the box, including hi-hat edge and rim zones, and MIDI learn covers any other kit. I use it with a Roland TD-4K.

Metronome

A click that sounds like a click

The default sound is a recording of a real mechanical metronome (CC0), with four takes rotated so repeats don't sound identical. Tempo ramps go 50 → 90 BPM in steps of 2, moving up only after four bars at 90%.

Counting coach

Count out loud, and get scored on it

My counting fell apart on the “&”. The app speaks a smooth “1 e & a” or “1 trip-let” at any tempo, then you count while it listens and marks each syllable early, late or missed. If talking isn't an option, tap a table or the screen instead.

Levels

Levels follow Rockschool grades

Level N means two things: you passed the app's Grade N tests at over 90% (Rockschool's Distinction line), at the book's tempos (♩=70 for Grades 1–2, ♩=75 for Grades 3–4), and you've put in the practice points behind it. Roughly 10,000 points per grade for 1–5 and 15,000 for 6–8, which works out to about half a year of 30-minute days per grade.

under 40%lose 1–5
40–60%0, learning zone
60–75% Pass1–3 points
75–90% Merit3–8 points
90%+ Dist.10–20

Playing an exercise to the end at 60% or better adds a finishing bonus of 2 to 10 points, depending on its length. Runs shorter than 10 seconds don't count either way, and a passed level test adds 50.

AI teacher

A weekly lesson plan through Notion and Claude

I don't have a real teacher, so this is the part I lean on most. The app doesn't call an AI model itself. It writes your practice history to a Notion page, and a Claude chat with access to that page acts as the teacher.

DrumCoach

Content

Your books stay yours

Stick Control and the Rockschool books are under copyright, so the app ships none of their content. Exercises come from four places:

Generated stickings

R/L permutations in the Stick Control style, built by the app. Combinations of R and L, not pages from the book.

A built-in curriculum

The app's own skill path toward Grade 8, plus the 40 PAS rudiments.

Your own books

Type an exercise in a short text format, or have Claude read a page of your PDF. Imports stay on the iPad.

Ear tests and dictation

Rockschool-style: hear a fill or groove twice, play it back, get scored.

DrumCoach is a native iPad app (Swift 6, SwiftUI, AVAudioEngine, CoreMIDI, SwiftData). The timing and scoring core is a separate Swift package with 366 tests.

It isn't on the App Store. It's built for one iPad, one 12" pad and one TD-4K. The demo above runs in your browser, so its timing is rougher than the app's.
